# Related work

The task here is to detect or estimate the change in the number of levels below a threshold for two Hermitian matrices related by a factored update. Both endpoint spectra avoid a known interval of half-width $\gamma$ around the threshold. The query model supplies normalized coherent block encodings of the base matrix and the positive and negative update factors. The calculation uses the total factor width $r$ and its smaller sign width $s$. With genuine spectral sign factors, these widths equal the total rank and the smaller sign rank.

## Spectral shift and classical inertia

The Birman–Solomyak spectral averaging formula expresses the spectral shift measure of a positive perturbation as an average of weighted spectral measures along a straight path. Simon's proof derives it by differentiating the trace of a function of the interpolating operator. In finite dimension, rank interlacing bounds the density by the rank of the update. The threshold gap makes the shift function constant in an interval. These facts supply the Gaussian integral identity and the smoothing estimate used here. [Simon, *Spectral averaging and the Krein spectral shift*](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-98-04261-0)

Classical low-rank eigenvalue algorithms already reduce a signed Hermitian modification to an inertia computation in the update coordinates. Arbenz, Gander and Golub use such a reduction to locate the eigenvalues of a modified matrix when the base spectral decomposition is known. Haynsworth's inertia formula explains the reduction directly. Write $B=A+WJW^\dagger$, with $W=[W_+\;W_-]$ and $J=\mathrm{diag}(I_{r_+},-I_{r_-})$, and move the threshold to zero. The Schur complements of the block matrix $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}A&W\\W^\dagger&-J\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ give

$$
S=J+W^\dagger A^{-1}W,\qquad
n_-(A)-n_-(B)=n_-(S)-r_-.
$$

The update-space inverse is $S^{-1}=J-JW^\dagger B^{-1}WJ$. In the normalized model, the two endpoint gaps therefore imply $\|S\|,\|S^{-1}\|=O(1/\gamma)$. A direct polynomial inverse followed by a polynomial sign on the resulting normalized $S$ uses a guaranteed sign gap of order $\gamma^2$. The straightforward nested construction has a filter-degree bound of $\widetilde O(\gamma^{-3})$ before estimating the trace. This comparison concerns that particular construction. Classical methods with an explicit base eigendecomposition have a different input cost. [Arbenz, Gander and Golub, *On the Spectral Decomposition of Hermitian Matrices Modified by Low Rank Perturbations with Applications*](https://doi.org/10.1137/0609004) · [Haynsworth, *Determination of the inertia of a partitioned Hermitian matrix*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(68)90050-5)

Gaussian filtering already appears in quantum eigenvalue estimation. QMEGS reconstructs peaks from controlled time evolutions and uses Gaussian filtering in postprocessing. QFAMES applies related filters to a matrix of transition amplitudes. Here the test itself uses a Gaussian contraction along a path, and the sequence of widths supplies coherent stopping times. The inverse-gap complexity bound follows from the survival probability of that stopped circuit. [Ding et al., *Quantum Multiple Eigenvalue Gaussian filtered Search*](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2024-10-02-1487) · [Ding, Lin, Yang and Zhang, *Quantum Filtering and Analysis of Multiplicities in Eigenvalue Spectra*](https://doi.org/10.1103/jch7-734h)

Quantum Spectral Readout studies the full weighted spectral measure of one Hamiltonian in Wasserstein-1 distance with fresh copies of its source state. Purity-Efficient Trace Estimation studies the normalized trace of a fixed unitary with a maximally mixed query register. The shift count uses coherent access to a supplied perturbation factor and a common gap for the two endpoints. [Quantum Spectral Readout](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Quantum-Spectral-Readout) · [Purity-Efficient Trace Estimation](https://github.com/GoGoKo699/Purity-Efficient-Trace-Estimation)

## Variable-time estimation and the lower bound

Quantum linear-system algorithms provide the closest precedent for the circuit organization. HHL applies a spectral inverse through eigenvalue-dependent operations. Ambainis introduced variable-time amplitude amplification to exploit branches with different stopping times, and Childs, Kothari and Somma combined variable timing with approximations of the inverse. Chakraborty, Gilyén and Jeffery's variable-time amplitude estimation theorem transfers relative success-probability estimation to this setting. The explicit Gaussian circuit uses Chebyshev walk powers and a linear combination of unitaries. Qubitization supplies the walk construction, and the real even-polynomial case of quantum singular value transformation gives another filter synthesis route. [Harrow, Hassidim and Lloyd](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.103.150502) · [Ambainis](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2012.636) · [Childs, Kothari and Somma](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1087072) · [Chakraborty, Gilyén and Jeffery, Theorem 23 in the long version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.01973) · [Low and Chuang, *Hamiltonian Simulation by Qubitization*](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2019-07-12-163) · [Gilyén et al., Corollary 18 in the long version](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.01838)

Approximate counting with bit queries has a long history, including the approximate-count lower bounds of Nayak and Wu and amplitude estimation by Brassard, Høyer, Mosca and Tapp. The lower-bound family here embeds weighted counting into normalized block encodings whose marked and unmarked completions differ in operator norm by $O(\gamma)$. The opposite-sign coordinates introduce a known cancellation level, so that detecting nonzero shift distinguishes adjacent Hamming weights near that level. This yields the joint dependence on rank, sign balance and gap. [Nayak and Wu](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9804066) · [Brassard et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0005055)

The algorithm uses the rank bound on spectral-shift density at **every** Gaussian width to control the root-mean-square stopping time. Splitting the signed update into two increasing paths makes both filtered signals nonnegative. Their difference cancels the common endpoint, which may have spectrum at the threshold. The smaller factor width bounds how much cancellation can occur. Together these properties give the detection crossover $\widetilde \Theta(\sqrt{r(s+1)}/\gamma)$ under the specified coherent query model. The additive estimation theorem gives $\widetilde O(r/[\gamma\max(1,\eta)])$ queries in the nontrivial accuracy range, with the corresponding lower bound away from the regime where a constant output suffices.
